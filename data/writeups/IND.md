A 33–30 loss in Kansas City that reads far better than the Week 1 blowout — Indianapolis went
into Arrowhead and traded blows, putting up 30 points on 329 yards against a real defense. The
headline is **Daniel Jones' health**: the rust and inaccuracy from his first game back off the
December Achilles tear are gone, and he looked genuinely mobile and in command moving the
offense to 30 on the road. That lifts both the quarterback and the surrounding offense.

- **QB Daniel Jones −0.5 → +0.5 (+1.0):** looks pretty healthy now; moved the ball to 30 in
  Kansas City.
- **Offense +0.5 → +0.8 (+0.3):** the above-average non-QB group (Taylor, the line, Pierce /
  Keenan Allen / Downs, Warren) produced a sustainable 30-point, 329-yard day on the road.
- **Defense −0.5 held:** still below the line, but that's fair against the caliber of offenses

## Week 1: vs Baltimore (L 23–41)

Indianapolis opened fast on a Jonathan Taylor touchdown drive, then got run off its own
field — outgained 506–251 in a 41–23 loss. As a near-pick'em (roughly a field-goal underdog),
getting smoked in all three phases is a real signal, so the rating comes down. Two things
soften it rather than sink it further: Daniel Jones was in his **first game back from a
December Achilles tear** and looked rusty (19-of-31, 166, 1 TD, 1 INT, nine hits absorbed),
and the opponent is elite. But the quarterback didn't look good and the defense had no answer.

## The case for a solid roster, marked down

Indianapolis still grades as a well-built roster held back by the quarterback spot — the
skill group is above average, but Week 1 pulled the quarterback and defense below the line.

## Quarterback

## What moved the number

- **Offense (+0.5, held):** unchanged — this is a genuinely above-average non-QB group and it
  was **not the problem** in Week 1. **Jonathan Taylor is an elite back** (98 yards, 2 TDs
  behind a strong line — Nelson, Raimann, Bortolini), with a real receiver trio (Pierce, Keenan
  Allen, Josh Downs) and second-year TE **Tyler Warren**.
- **Defense (0.0 → −0.5):** cut after an awful showing — no pressure, no stops, gashed for 506
  yards and 41 points. The **Sauce Gardner / Charvarius Ward** secondary is still a real upgrade
  on paper, but the unit made no impact, and the linebacking and pass rush looked overmatched.

## Bottom line

A quietly complete roster; the ceiling is capped by Daniel Jones, but the surrounding talent — Taylor, the line, and the upgraded secondary — makes this better than a bottom-half team.
