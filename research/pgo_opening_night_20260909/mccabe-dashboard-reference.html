2 Buffalo BillsJosh Allen +6.5+0.6-0.5 +6.6 ›

The case near the top

Buffalo's rating is a quarterback story. Josh Allen at +6.5 is the model's ceiling grade, and he drags an otherwise good-not-great roster into the top tier all by himself.

Quarterback

Allen (+6.5) is the engine — an MVP-caliber dual threat and the top QB grade in the model. He props up the whole offense, which is exactly why the roster still rates near the top even after the non-QB units were marked down.

What moved the number

  • Offense (+0.6): trimmed from +1.0 — a good-not-elite group that leans heavily on Allen. DJ Moore is a real WR1 add, but the room thins out behind him: Keon Coleman is working back from a sprained right foot/toe (Aug 15; out of the walking boot and practicing, expected to play Week 1) and Khalil Shakir is working through an ankle injury (reported high-ankle sprain, "week-to-week"). Add the losses of Cooks, Davis, and Samuel and the pass-catching cast is more solid than special. Two things keep this from grading lower: the offensive line is a genuine strength, and James Cook is one of the top-tier running backs in the league — a real lever the receiver questions understate.
  • Defense (-0.5): below average, and we expect it to be worse than last year's version. Buffalo moved on from Sean McDermott (the franchise is now led by HC Joe Brady); even though we were never high on McDermott, his defense graded better than what we expect coming out of this transition. New coordinator Jim Leonhard — in his first NFL coordinator job — is installing an aggressive, attacking hybrid 3-4 built on pre-snap disguises and pressure packages, a real departure from McDermott's zone-heavy system. A brand-new scheme, a first-time NFL play-caller, and last year's run-defense and missed-tackle problems add up to real transition risk. Losing Bosa and Milano thins the front further; CGJ, Chubb, and Stone are the counters, and a Mathis suspension hurts the interior depth.

Risk

This is a top-tier rating built almost entirely on one MVP-level input. With the receiver room down to DJ Moore plus two banged-up complementary starters and a defense in scheme transition, there's little margin if Allen has a merely ordinary year — the number climbs back toward its old spot if the pass-catchers get healthy and the new-look defense settles in faster than expected.
